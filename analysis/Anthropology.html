2016-2019

Underlying Patterns and Repeating Questions in Anthropology Exams (2016–2019)

1. Core Definitions and Scope of Anthropology

  1. Repeating Questions:
  2. Define anthropology and differentiate it from other social sciences (2016 Q2, 2018 Q2).
  3. Discuss the fields/branches of anthropology (2019 Q2).
  4. Pattern: Questions on foundational definitions, scope, and uniqueness of anthropology recur every 2–3 years.

2. Religion and Evolution

  1. Repeating Questions:
  2. Define religion and its functions; discuss primitive religion (2016 Q6).
  3. Tylor’s evolutionary scheme of religion and animism (2017 Q5).
  4. Compare divine religions with others (2019 Q7).
  5. Pattern: Tylor’s theories and religion’s role in society are tested almost annually.

3. Social Change and Applied Anthropology

  1. Repeating Questions:
  2. Socio-cultural barriers to change (2016 Q3).
  3. Applied anthropology in solving contemporary problems (2017 Q3).
  4. Factors responsible for social change in simple societies (2019 Q4).
  5. Pattern: Focus on dynamics of change, barriers, and practical applications.

4. Research Methodology

  1. Repeating Questions:
  2. Anthropological research techniques; qualitative vs. quantitative (2016 Q4).
  3. Objectivity vs. subjectivity in research (2018 Q3).
  4. Technique vs. method vs. methodology (2019 Q5).
  5. Pattern: Annual emphasis on research design, tools, and epistemological debates.

5. Kinship, Family, and Marriage Systems

  1. Repeating Questions:
  2. Endogamy, exogamy, consanguine/affine relatives (2016 Q8).
  3. Kinship systems and political allegiances (2018 Q7).
  4. Family types (structure, residence, succession) (2019 Q6).
  5. Pattern: Kinship terms and family structures appear in short notes and essays yearly.

6. Economic Systems

  1. Repeating Questions:
  2. Evolution of economic systems (reciprocity → redistribution → market) (2018 Q5).
  3. Primitive economic organization (2019 Q3).
  4. Pattern: Economic anthropology, especially evolutionary models, recurs every 1–2 years.

7. Contemporary Issues in Pakistan

  1. Repeating Questions:
  2. Contemporary social problems of Pakistan (2016 Q5).
  3. Sectarianism and national integration (2018 Q6).
  4. Poverty vs. population pressure debate (2017 Q7).
  5. Pattern: Pakistan-specific societal issues are tested every 1–2 years.

8. Cultural Concepts

  1. Repeating Questions:
  2. Define culture and its characteristics (2016 Q2).
  3. Cultural relativism (2018 Q8).
  4. Culture responding to human needs (2018 Q4).
  5. Pattern: Core cultural theories and relativism are frequent themes.

9. Short Notes on Key Terms

  1. Repeating Terms:
  2. Kinship: Sororate, virilocal residence (2017, 2018).
  3. Research Tools: Participant observation (2018, 2019).
  4. Cultural Terms: Ethnocentrism (2016), cultural relativism (2018).
  5. Pattern: 4–5 short notes per year, often overlapping terms across years.

10. Theories and Theorists

  1. Repeating Topics:
  2. Morgan & Tylor’s social evolution (2016 Q7).
  3. Tylor’s animism (2017 Q5).
  4. Pattern: Classic 19th-century evolutionary theorists are frequently referenced.

Key Takeaways

  1. High-Frequency Topics: Religion (Tylor), kinship systems, research methods, Pakistan’s social issues, and cultural definitions.
  2. Recurring Formats: Short notes on terminology, comparative essays (e.g., qualitative vs. quantitative), and application-based questions (e.g., solving global issues through anthropology).
  3. Theoretical Focus: Evolutionary theories (Morgan, Tylor) and debates on objectivity in research.

Years with Overlapping Questions:

  1. 2016 & 2018: Anthropology’s definition vs. social sciences.
  2. 2016 & 2017: Tylor’s theories on religion.
  3. 2017 & 2019: Economic systems and kinship terms.
