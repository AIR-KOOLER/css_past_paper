2001-2005

Underlying Patterns and Repeating Questions in Statistics Exams (2001–2005)

1. Probability Theory

  • Recurring Topics:
    • Classical, axiomatic, and relative frequency definitions of probability (2001, 2004).
    • Law of total probability, Bayes’ theorem, conditional probability (2001, 2003, 2004).
    • Mutually exclusive vs. independent events (2001, 2003, 2004).
  • Examples:
    • 2001 Q1a: Definitions of probability with examples.
    • 2004 Q1c: Proof that mutually exclusive events cannot be independent.

2. Probability Distributions

  • Key Distributions:
    • Normal: Properties (mean = median = mode), applications (2001, 2002, 2003, 2004, 2005).
    • Binomial vs. Poisson: Comparisons, variance derivations (2001, 2002, 2004, 2005).
    • Negative Binomial/Hypergeometric: Definitions, mean/variance (2001, 2003, 2004).
  • Examples:
    • 2003 Q3a: Hypergeometric probabilities summing to unity.
    • 2005 Q8(3): Binomial distribution properties (mean vs. variance).

3. Hypothesis Testing

  • Common Tests:
    • t-test/z-test: Comparing means (2003, 2004, 2005).
    • Chi-square: Independence, goodness-of-fit (2001, 2002, 2005).
    • ANOVA: Testing equality of means (2002, 2004, 2005).
  • Examples:
    • 2001 Q7: Chi-square test for marital status and absenteeism.
    • 2005 Q3b: t-test for pedestrian fatalities across time periods.

4. Regression & Correlation

  • Key Topics:
    • Regression coefficients, interpretation of slope/intercept (2001, 2003, 2004, 2005).
    • Coefficient of determination ((r^2)) and partial correlation (2002, 2004, 2005).
  • Examples:
    • 2001 Q5a: Regression line definitions and relationship with correlation.
    • 2004 Q4a: Calculating correlation coefficient and regression line.

5. Sampling Techniques

  • Frequent Methods:
    • Stratified, cluster, systematic sampling (2001, 2002, 2003, 2004, 2005).
    • Comparisons: Stratum vs. cluster, precision vs. reliability (2004, 2005).
  • Examples:
    • 2002 Q5: Advantages of cluster sampling.
    • 2005 Q5a: Procedure for stratified random sampling.

6. Statistical Inference

  • Key Concepts:
    • Maximum likelihood estimation (2001, 2003, 2004).
    • Properties of estimators (unbiasedness, consistency) (2004, 2005).
  • Examples:
    • 2001 Q4b: MLE for exponential distribution.
    • 2004 Q3b: Desirable properties of point estimators.

7. Compulsory MCQ/True-False Sections

  • Repeated Concepts:
    • Standard error (2004 Q8(1), Poisson mean = variance (2004 Q8(3)).
    • Skewness (2004 Q8(6): Mean > median > mode in positive skew.
    • ANOVA assumptions (2003 Q8e, 2004 Q6b).
  • Examples:
    • 2003 Q8: "Random variable is always a real-valued function" (True).
    • 2005 Q8(7): "Coefficient of determination = (r^2)" (True).

8. Short Notes

  • Frequent Topics:
    • ANOVA, Bayes’ theorem, multiple/partial correlation (2002, 2003, 2004, 2005).
    • Disadvantages of sampling, normal distribution importance (2002, 2004).

Summary of Patterns

  1. Core Focus: Probability, distributions (normal, binomial, Poisson), hypothesis testing, regression, and sampling methods dominate all exams.
  2. Application Emphasis: Real-world scenarios (public health, socio-economics, manufacturing) are common in questions.
  3. Yearly Structure: Compulsory MCQs test foundational concepts, while long questions blend theory and application.
  4. Repeat Topics:
    1. Normal distribution properties (all years).
    2. Regression line interpretation (2001–2005).
    3. Stratified vs. cluster sampling (2001–2005).

Underlying Patterns and Repeating Questions in CSS Statistics Exams (2020–2024)

Key Takeaways for Preparation

  1. Master Regression Analysis: Focus on interpreting ( r^2 ), computing coefficients, and hypothesis testing for slopes.
  2. Practice Probability Scenarios: Urn problems, conditional probability, and hypergeometric/Poisson distributions.
  3. Understand Sampling Distributions: Variance formulas and proofs (especially for finite populations).
  4. ANOVA and Experimental Design: RCBD, Latin Square, LSD test applications.
  5. Index Numbers: Memorize Laspeyres, Paasche, and Fisher’s formulas.
  6. Hypothesis Testing: T-tests, Z-tests, and confidence intervals for proportions/means.
