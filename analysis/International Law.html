2014-2018

Underlying Patterns and Repeating Questions in International Law Exams (2014–2018)

Note: Topics marked with ★ indicate recurring themes across multiple years.

1. Sources of International Law ★★★

  1. Key Questions:2014 Q2: Consent as the basis of international law and sources.
  2. 2015 Q3: Sources per ICJ Statute Article 38(1).
  3. 2016 Q3: Material sources (e.g., treaties, customs).
  4. 2017 Q3: Primary vs. Subsidiary sources.
  5. 2018 Q2: "General principles" vs. positivism.

Pattern:

  1. Focus on Article 38(1) of the ICJ Statute (treaties, customs, general principles).
  2. Debates around consent vs. positivism and the role of state practice.

2. State Recognition and Sovereignty ★★★

  1. Key Questions:2015 Q6: State recognition and existence.
  2. 2016 Q7: De jure vs. de facto recognition; constitutive vs. declaratory theories.
  3. 2017 Q7: Self-determination and Montevideo Convention.
  4. 2018 Q4: Recognition as "fluid and inconsistent state practice" (Starke).

Pattern:

  1. Montevideo Convention criteria (population, territory, government, capacity).
  2. Distinctions between de jure/de facto recognition and legal consequences.

3. United Nations and Security Council ★★★

  1. Key Questions:2014 Q6: Security Council composition.
  2. 2017 Q4: Good offices/mediation vs. Security Council’s "highhandedness."
  3. 2018 Q5: Security Council powers under UN Charter Chapters VI and VII.
  4. 2018 Q6: General Assembly vs. Security Council in dispute resolution.
  5. 2018 Q7: Recurring issue of Security Council composition reform.

Pattern:

  1. Chapters VI (peaceful resolution) and VII (enforcement) of the UN Charter.
  2. Critiques of veto power and demands for equitable representation.

4. Treaties and Conventions ★★

  1. Key Questions:2015 Q5: Formation, interpretation, and termination of treaties.
  2. 2017 Q6: Vienna Convention on treaties (definition, features, termination).
  3. 2018 Q8: UN’s role despite imperfections (linked to treaty-based systems).

Pattern:

  1. Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties (1969) as a central reference.
  2. Focus on pacta sunt servanda and grounds for termination (e.g., material breach).

5. Jurisdiction and State Responsibilities ★★

  1. Key Questions:2014 Q3: Territorial sea limits and principles (acquiescence/opposability).
  2. 2016 Q4: Freedom of High Seas vs. coastal state rights (hot pursuit).
  3. 2016 Q5: Types of state jurisdiction (territorial, nationality, protective).
  4. 2015 Q4: Diplomatic immunities vs. privileges.

Pattern:

  1. UNCLOS (1982) principles (territorial sea, EEZ, hot pursuit).
  2. Jurisdictional conflicts (e.g., Lotus Case, 2016 Q8a).

6. Theoretical Debates ★★

  1. Key Questions:2015 Q2: Natural Law vs. Positive Law theories.
  2. 2017 Q2: "International Law is oxymoronic" (effectiveness debate).
  3. 2018 Q2: General principles vs. positivism.

Pattern:

  1. Positivism vs. natural law in shaping international norms.
  2. Critiques of IL’s enforceability and relevance.

7. Case Studies and Short Notes ★★

  1. Recurring Cases:Corfu Channel Case (2015 Q8a).
  2. Lotus Case (2016 Q8a).
  3. Nottebohm Case (2015 Q8c, 2016 Q7).
  4. Rainbow Warrior Case (2015 Q8b).

Pattern:

  1. Focus on landmark ICJ cases to illustrate principles (e.g., state responsibility, recognition).

8. Specialized Topics (Single Occurrences)

  1. 2014 Q8: ILO as a model for organizations.
  2. 2017 Q5: Treatment of POWs (Geneva Conventions).
  3. 2017 Q8c: Collective use of force (UN Charter Article 42).

Summary of Key Recurring Themes

  1. Sources of IL (every year).
  2. UN Security Council (composition, powers, reform).
  3. State Recognition (criteria, theories).
  4. Treaty Law (Vienna Convention).
  5. Jurisdictional Principles (territory, high seas, diplomatic immunities).

Exam Strategy:

  1. Master Article 38(1) ICJ Statute and Vienna Convention.
  2. Prepare critiques of UNSC structure and state recognition theories.
  3. Study key cases (e.g., Lotus, Nottebohm) and their legal principles.

2019-2024

Underlying Patterns and Repeating Questions in International Law Exams (2019–2024)

Below are recurring themes and questions across the years, along with the specific exam years and question numbers where they appeared:

**1. Monism vs. Dualism

  1. Years & Questions:
  2. 2019 (Q3): Distinguish between Monism and Dualism. Discuss incorporation/transformation.
  3. 2020 (Q3): Examine how states’ adherence to monism/dualism shapes their approach to international law.
  4. Pattern: Focus on theoretical frameworks for domestic adoption of international law, modalities (incorporation/transformation), and practical implications for state practice.

**2. Origin and Nature of International Law

  1. Years & Questions:
  2. 2019 (Q2): Difficulty in historical accounts of international law’s origin.
  3. 2022 (Q2): Historical account of origin and relevance in modern times.
  4. 2024 (Q5a): Debate on whether international law is "law" or "positive morality."
  5. Pattern: Recurring focus on historical development, critiques of international law’s legitimacy (Austin vs. modern enforceability), and relevance in contemporary contexts.

**3. Subjects of International Law (States vs. Non-State Entities)

  1. Years & Questions:
  2. 2022 (Q3): States as principal subjects; justification of individuals as subjects.
  3. 2023 (Q2): Influence of non-state entities on international law.
  4. 2024 (Q4): States as original subjects; advantages/disadvantages of their primacy.
  5. Pattern: Emphasis on states’ legal personality, challenges posed by non-state actors (e.g., NGOs, armed groups), and evolving roles of individuals.

**4. Jurisdiction (Territorial, Prescriptive, Enforcement)

  1. Years & Questions:
  2. 2019 (Q4): Territorial jurisdiction over crimes.
  3. 2021 (Q4): Self-defense against non-state armed groups.
  4. 2024 (Q3a): Prescriptive jurisdiction types; Q3b: Enforcement jurisdiction (Lotus Case).
  5. Pattern: Questions on territorial sovereignty, enforcement jurisdiction (e.g., Lotus Case), and modern challenges like terrorism and cybercrimes.

**5. Use of Force and Self-Defense

  1. Years & Questions:
  2. 2019 (Q8): Article 2(4) prohibition and exceptions.
  3. 2023 (Q4): Customary law and Article 51 (terrorist attacks).
  4. 2024 (Q2a): Self-defense under Article 51 vs. customary law (Caroline incident).
  5. Pattern: Analysis of UN Charter provisions (Articles 2(4), 51), state practice, and debates on preemptive/anticipatory self-defense.

**6. International Court of Justice (ICJ)

  1. Years & Questions:
  2. 2021 (Q6): ICJ’s jurisdiction and compulsory jurisdiction debate.
  3. 2022 (Q6): ICJ’s structure, precedents, and role.
  4. 2024 (Q6): Advisory vs. contentious jurisdiction (Palestinian Wall opinion).
  5. Pattern: Focus on ICJ’s jurisdiction types (consent-based vs. advisory), enforcement challenges, and landmark cases.

**7. State Responsibility and Reparation

  1. Years & Questions:
  2. 2019 (Q5): Imputability of insurrectional groups’ conduct to states.
  3. 2023 (Q7): Constituent elements of state responsibility; Draft Code of 2001.
  4. Pattern: Emphasis on attribution of wrongful acts, forms of reparation (restitution, compensation), and state accountability.

**8. Recognition in International Law

  1. Years & Questions:
  2. 2022 (Q5): Constitutive vs. declaratory theories; consequences of non-recognition.
  3. 2024 (Q4a): Examples of states’ legal personality (linked to recognition).
  4. Pattern: Theories of recognition, legal effects of recognition/non-recognition, and case studies (e.g., Kosovo, Taiwan).

**9. Treaties and Customary Law

  1. Years & Questions:
  2. 2023 (Q5): Definition of treaties (Vienna Convention 1969).
  3. 2024 (Q7a): Importance of treaties; Q7b): Treaty formation steps.
  4. Pattern: Focus on treaty formation (VCLT), jus cogens, and interaction between treaties and customary law.

**10. Short Notes on Recurring Topics

  1. Frequent Topics:
  2. Diplomatic Immunity (2020 Q8b, 2021 Q8, 2024 Q8a).
  3. Asylum (Territorial/Extra-Territorial) (2022 Q8b, 2023 Q5ii).
  4. Cases (Nottebohm, Lotus) (2022 Q8c, 2024 Q3b).
  5. IHL Principles (Perfidy/Ruses, Blockade) (2020 Q5, 2024 Q8c).

Key Observations:

  1. High-Frequency Topics:
  2. Monism/Dualism, ICJ jurisdiction, use of force, state responsibility, and subjects of international law.
  3. Emerging Trends:
  4. Increased focus on non-state actors (terrorist groups, NGOs) and modern challenges (cyber warfare, terrorism).
  5. Case Law:
  6. Repeated references to landmark cases: Lotus Case, Caroline Incident, Nottebohm Case, ICJ Advisory Opinions.
  7. UN Charter Articles:
  8. Articles 2(4), 51, and Chapter VII provisions are frequently tested.

Study Priority: Focus on foundational theories (Monism/Dualism), ICJ, use of force, state responsibility, and recurring cases. Short notes topics (diplomatic immunity, asylum) should also be thoroughly prepared.
