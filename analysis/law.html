Part I: Civil Procedure Code (CPC), 1908

The CPC is a cornerstone of civil litigation in Pakistan, and exam questions consistently test foundational procedural concepts. A strong grasp of the principles governing jurisdiction, pleadings, and the finality of judgments is essential.

1.1 Res Judicata and Res Sub-Judice

This distinction is one of the most frequently tested topics across all years.

  • Res Sub-Judice (Section 10, CPC): This doctrine means "a matter under consideration". It prevents a court from trying a suit where the matter in issue is already pending in a previously instituted suit between the same parties. The primary objective is to prevent courts of concurrent jurisdiction from simultaneously adjudicating two parallel litigations, which could lead to conflicting verdicts and unnecessary harassment of litigants. For this rule to apply, the matter in issue must be directly and substantially the same in both suits.
  • Res Judicata (Section 11, CPC): This doctrine means "a matter already decided". It bars the trial of a suit or issue that has already been finally decided in a former suit between the same parties. The principle is based on the idea that a judicial decision must be accepted as correct and that there should be an end to litigation.

1.2 Pleadings (Order VI, VII, VIII)

Pleadings are the written statements of the plaintiff (plaint) and the defendant (written statement) that form the foundation of a civil suit.

  • Fundamental Rules: Pleadings must contain a concise statement of material facts only, not the evidence by which those facts are to be proved. They should state facts, not law. The purpose is to define the scope of the controversy and give the opposing party notice of the case they have to meet.
  • Amendment of Pleadings (Order VI, Rule 17): Courts may allow parties to alter or amend their pleadings at any stage of the proceedings to determine the real questions in controversy. However, amendments are generally allowed before the settlement of issues and only in exceptional circumstances thereafter.

1.3 Execution of Decrees (Sections 36-74, Order XXI)

Execution is the final stage of litigation, where the judgment of the court is enforced.

  • Executing Court: A decree can be executed either by the court that passed it or by a court to which it is sent for execution. The executing court cannot go behind the decree or question its legality.
  • Modes of Execution: A decree may be executed by the delivery of property, by attachment and sale of property, or by the arrest and detention of the judgment-debtor.
  • Foreign Judgments: The enforcement of foreign judgments is governed by Sections 13 and 44-A of the CPC. A decree from a "reciprocating territory" can be directly executed in Pakistan by filing an execution application, while others require a separate suit to be filed.

Part II: Contract Act, 1872

The Contract Act forms the basis of commercial and civil transactions. Exam questions consistently test the foundational principles of what makes an agreement legally enforceable.

2.1 Core Principles of Contract Law

  • Essentials of a Valid Contract: Questions on the essential elements of a valid contract (offer, acceptance, consideration, capacity, free consent, lawful object) are a recurring theme.
  • Void vs. Voidable Contracts: A clear understanding of the distinction between void agreements (which are not enforceable by law) and voidable contracts (enforceable at the option of one party) is critical. This includes topics like contracts with minors and the effect of coercion, undue influence, and fraud.
  • Specific Contracts: Concepts such as bailment, tender, and contingent contracts are also frequently tested.

Curated Resources for Contract Act

  • Books and Guides:
    • Standard textbooks on the Contract Act, 1872 are indispensable.
    • General law exam guides, such as those from Dogar Brothers and Jahangir World Times, often contain sections on contract law.
  • Online Resources:
    • Websites like The CSS Point provide access to past papers, which are crucial for understanding question patterns.
  • Video Lectures:
    • General legal education channels on YouTube often cover the essentials of the Contract Act as part of their curriculum for Pakistani law students.

Part III: Law of Torts

The Law of Torts deals with civil wrongs. Exam questions in this area focus on specific torts like negligence and defamation, as well as general principles like liability.

3.1 Key Areas in Tort Law

  • Negligence: This includes the essentials of negligence (duty of care, breach, causation, damages), the burden of proof, and defenses such as contributory negligence.
  • Defamation: The distinction between libel (written) and slander (spoken) is a high-frequency topic.
  • Liability: Questions on vicarious liability (employer's liability for acts of an employee) and strict liability (the rule in Rylands v. Fletcher) are common.

Curated Resources for Law of Torts

  • Books and Guides:
    • Law of Torts by Ratanlal & Dhirajlal is a classic text.
    • Guides for competitive exams often include dedicated sections on tort law.
  • Online Resources:
    • Legal education websites often provide notes and case summaries on key tort law principles.
  • Video Lectures:
    • Many Pakistani law channels on YouTube offer lectures on negligence, defamation, and other core torts.

Part IV: Criminal Law (PPC, 1860 & CrPC, 1898)

Criminal law questions test both substantive offenses under the Pakistan Penal Code (PPC) and the procedural rules under the Criminal Procedure Code (CrPC).

4.1 Pakistan Penal Code (PPC)

  • General Principles: The concept of mens rea (guilty mind) and the general exceptions to criminal liability, especially the Right of Private Defence, are fundamental and frequently tested.
  • Offences Against the Human Body: Distinctions between culpable homicide and murder, and concepts like Qisas and Diyat, are high-weightage topics.
  • Offences Against Property: The distinctions between theft, extortion, robbery, and dacoity are a recurring theme.

4.2 Criminal Procedure Code (CrPC)

  • Initiation of Proceedings: The difference between a First Information Report (FIR) and a private complaint is a critical procedural distinction tested repeatedly.
  • Pre-Trial Procedures: The law relating to arrest without a warrant and the principles governing the grant of bail in bailable and non-bailable offenses are essential.
  • Trial Procedures: Questions on the hierarchy of criminal courts, the process of summary trials, and the distinction between appeal, revision, and review are common.

Curated Resources for Criminal Law

  • Bare Acts: The primary resource for preparation should be the Bare Acts of the PPC and CrPC.
  • Online Resources:
    • The Law GAT syllabus provides a detailed breakdown of important sections from both codes.
    • Websites like The CSS Point offer past papers for Criminology, which can provide additional context.
  • Video Lectures:
    • YouTube channels like "Basic Pakistani Laws in Urdu" provide accessible explanations of criminal law concepts.

Part V: Qanun-e-Shahadat Order (QSO), 1984

The QSO governs the rules of evidence in Pakistan. Exam questions focus on the types of evidence and the principles of proof.

5.1 Key Concepts in Evidence Law

  • Types of Evidence: The distinction between oral and documentary evidence, and primary versus secondary evidence, is fundamental. The rules regarding hearsay evidence and its exceptions are also important.
  • Confessions and Admissions: Understanding when a confession is admissible, the procedural safeguards for recording it, and how it differs from an admission is a high-frequency topic.
  • Burden of Proof: Candidates must know the general principles of burden of proof and how it shifts in civil and criminal cases.

Curated Resources for QSO

  • Bare Act: The primary text is the Qanun-e-Shahadat Order, 1984 itself.
  • Online Resources:
    • The Law GAT syllabus outlines the key articles of the QSO to focus on.
  • Video Lectures:
    • YouTube channels like "Study Law With Bushra" and "Theory Of Law" offer dedicated lectures and MCQ sessions on the QSO.

Part VI: Arbitration and ADR

With a growing emphasis on alternative dispute resolution, questions on arbitration have become more frequent in recent years.

6.1 Core Topics in Arbitration

  • Arbitration Agreement: The nature and validity of arbitration agreements.
  • Arbitrators: The procedure for the appointment and removal of arbitrators.
  • Arbitral Awards: The grounds for setting aside an arbitral award and the procedure for its enforcement.
  • International Commercial Arbitration (ICA): An understanding of the international framework is increasingly important.

Curated Resources for Arbitration

  • Books and Guides:
    • Textbooks on the Arbitration Act, 1940, and international commercial arbitration are recommended.
  • Online Resources:
    • Legal blogs and websites of law firms often publish articles on recent developments in arbitration law in Pakistan.
  • Video Lectures:
    • Advanced legal lecture series may cover topics related to ADR and arbitration.

General Study Materials and Strategy

A successful preparation strategy involves more than just subject-specific knowledge. It requires understanding the exam's structure, the examiner's expectations, and consistent practice.

  • Past Papers: Analyzing past papers is the single most important strategy. It helps in understanding question patterns, identifying high-frequency topics, and improving time management. Resources like The CSS Point and other online archives provide access to a wide range of past papers.
  • Guide Books and MCQs: For the objective portion of exams like the Law GAT and other screening tests, using guidebooks with solved MCQs is essential. Publishers like Dogar Brothers and Jahangir World Times are popular choices.
  • Video Lectures and Online Courses: Platforms like YouTube and dedicated academies offer lectures, past paper solutions, and mock tests that can significantly aid preparation.
  • Bare Acts: For any law exam, the primary text is always the Bare Act of the relevant statute. A thorough reading is non-negotiable.

By focusing on these high-yield topics and utilizing the recommended resources, you can build a strong foundation and develop the analytical skills necessary to excel in your law examinations.
