Recommended Books for CSS Subjects

Compulsory Subjects

English Essay

Better Writing Now by Francine D. Galko

 – A guide focused on improving writing skills and essay

composition. 
How to Write Good Essays by Lauren Starkey

 – Practical strategies for structuring and writing

effective essays for exams.

English (Precis & Composition)

High School English Grammar and Composition by Wren & Martin

 – Comprehensive grammar rules

and composition exercises to strengthen writing fundamentals. 
Exploring the World of English by Syed Saadat Ali Shah

 – Covers vocabulary, grammar, and various

composition forms, tailored for competitive exams.

General Science & Ability

General Science and Ability by Mian Shafiq (JWT)

 – Covers a broad range of general science topics

and basic arithmetic/analytical ability as per the CSS syllabus. 
General Science and Ability by Prof. Muhammad Akram Kashmiri

 – Another comprehensive book

addressing both science fundamentals and general ability (quantitative and logical reasoning) for
CSS.

Current Affairs

Pakistan’s Foreign Policy 1947–2016: A Concise History by Abdul Sattar

 – An insightful overview of

Pakistan’s foreign relations and policy, helpful for understanding current affairs in historical context. 
Pakistan: A Hard Country by Anatol Lieven

 – Provides deep analysis of Pakistan’s political and

social structures, offering context for current issues.

(In addition to books, regularly reading quality newspapers like  Dawn, and magazines such as  The Economist, 
Foreign Affairs, etc., is highly recommended for Current Affairs preparation.)

Pakistan Affairs

Constitutional and Political History of Pakistan by Hamid Khan

 – A detailed account of Pakistan’s

constitutional developments and political events from 1947 onward. 
Pakistan: Beyond the Crisis State edited by Maleeha Lodhi

 – A collection of essays analyzing the

challenges and prospects for Pakistan in various domains (political, economic, social).

Islamic Studies

Islam: Its Meaning and Message by Khurshid Ahmad

 – Covers fundamental Islamic concepts, the

message of Islam, and its guiding principles. 
Islamic Studies (Islamiyat) by Hafiz Karimdad Chughtai

 – A book structured according to the CSS

Islamic Studies syllabus, covering a wide range of topics with references to Qur’an and Hadith.

Comparative Study of Major Religions (For Non-Muslims)

Approaches to the Study of Religion (ed. Peter Connolly)

 – Introduces various methodologies and

perspectives for studying world religions. 
The Penguin Handbook of the World’s Living Religions (ed. John R. Hinnells)

 – Provides an overview

of major world religions (Hinduism, Buddhism, Judaism, Christianity, Islam, etc.), their beliefs and
practices, in an accessible form.

Optional Subjects

(Each candidate must choose subjects totaling 600 marks from the optional groups as per CSS rules. Below are
recommended books for each optional subject, grouped by the CSS subject groups I–VII.)

Group I

Auditing & Accounting

Principles of Accounting by Sohail Afzal

 – Covers financial accounting and the basics of accounting

principles; useful for both Paper I and II of Accountancy. 
Advanced Auditing by Prof. Dr. Khawaja Amjad Saeed

 – Comprehensive guide on auditing

standards, techniques, and practices, covering the auditing portion of the syllabus.

Economics

The Economy of Pakistan by Khawaja Amjad Saeed

 – Details Pakistan’s economic structure and

issues, including agriculture, industry, and economic policies. 
Issues in Pakistan’s Economy by Akbar S. Zaidi

 – Analyzes economic challenges and developments

in Pakistan; a crucial book for understanding Pakistan’s economy historically and in the present.

(For theory portions in micro and macro economics, textbooks by Mankiw for introductory economics and Todaro
for development economics are also beneficial

.)

Computer Science

C++ How to Program by Harvey M. Deitel & Paul Deitel

 – Useful for programming fundamentals

and object-oriented concepts in C++ (covers part of the programming portion of the syllabus). 
Operating System Concepts by Abraham Silberschatz, Peter Galvin, and Greg Gagne

 – A standard

textbook covering operating system principles, which aligns with the OS portion of the CSS
Computer Science syllabus.

Political Science

Political Thought: From Plato to the Present by M. Judd Harmon

 – Provides an overview of Western

political thought and theories, covering ancient to modern philosophers. 
Political Science: Theory and Practice by Mazhar-ul-Haq

 – A comprehensive book on political

science concepts, state theories, and the functioning of political systems, with context relevant to
Pakistan.

International Relations

International Relations by Joshua S. Goldstein

 – A widely recommended IR textbook covering key

theories, concepts of power, war, peace, international political economy, and global issues. 
The Globalization of World Politics by John Baylis, Steve Smith, & Patricia Owens

 – An excellent

compilation covering fundamental concepts, theories, and contemporary issues in IR through
chapters written by subject experts.

(Other useful IR references include World Politics: Trend and Transformation by Charles W. Kegley

 and How

the World Works: A Brief Survey of International Relations by Russell Bova

. For Pakistan-specific context,

Pakistan’s Foreign Policy by Abdul Sattar is also valuable.)

Group II

Physics

Fundamentals of Physics by Halliday, Resnick & Krane

 – A foundational physics textbook covering

mechanics, electromagnetism, thermodynamics, etc., suitable for conceptual clarity and problem
practice. 
Concepts of Modern Physics by Arthur Beiser

 – Focuses on modern physics topics (quantum

theory, atomic structure, nuclear physics) which are part of the higher-level physics syllabus.

(For certain topics, refer to specialized sources: e.g. Electricity and Magnetism by Sadiku for electromagnetics

, Thermodynamics by Eastop & McConkey

 for thermodynamics, and other standard university-level texts

as needed.)

Chemistry

Physical Chemistry by Silbey, Alberty & Bawendi

 – Covers thermodynamics, kinetics, quantum

chemistry and other physical chemistry principles in detail. 
Organic Chemistry by Morrison & Boyd

 – A classic text for understanding organic chemistry

mechanisms and reactions, covering the organic portion of the syllabus.

(Additionally, Concise Inorganic Chemistry by J.D. Lee is often recommended for inorganic chemistry topics, and
solving past papers is crucial for this subject.)

Applied Mathematics

An Introduction to Vector Analysis by Dr. Khalid Latif

 – Provides coverage of vector algebra and

calculus, a key area in the Applied Mathematics syllabus. 
Introduction to Mechanics by Q.K. Ghori

 – Addresses dynamics and statics, covering classical

mechanics problems relevant to the applied math portion.

(Other   useful   texts   include   Khalid   Latif’s  An   Intermediate   Course   in   Theoretical   Mechanics  for   advanced
mechanics

  and  Differential Equations with Boundary Value Problems  by Zill & Cullen for the differential

equations part of the syllabus.)

Pure Mathematics

Calculus (with Analytic Geometry) by Howard Anton, Irl Bivens & Stephen Davis

 – A thorough

calculus text covering single and multivariable calculus, sequences and series, etc., which are
fundamental for Pure Mathematics. 
Advanced Calculus by Wilfred Kaplan

 – Explores advanced topics in calculus and analysis, useful

for higher-level calculus and real analysis portions of the syllabus.

(For abstract algebra and other topics, one may consult Topics in Algebra by I.N. Herstein or similar texts. Past
paper practice helps identify which areas of pure math to focus on.)

Statistics

Introduction to Statistical Theory by Sher Muhammad Chaudhry and Shahid Kamal – This two-part
book (Part I & II) is a staple for covering probability, distributions, and statistical inference as per CSS
syllabus (commonly used in M.A./M.Sc. statistics as well). 
Probability and Statistics for Engineers and Scientists by Ronald E. Walpole et al.

 – A helpful

reference for probability theory, descriptive statistics, and inferential techniques, explained in an
intuitive way.

(It’s also useful to cover topics from Principles and Procedures of Statistics by Steel & Torrie for experimental
statistics and to practice exercises from past CSS papers and FPSC-recommended problem sets

.)

Geology

Physical Geology by Charles C. Plummer, David McGeary & Diane Carlson

 – An introductory text

covering Earth’s structure, minerals, rocks, geological processes, and landscape development. 
Principles of Physical Geology by Arthur Holmes – A classic book providing a strong foundation in
physical geology, including plate tectonics, earth materials, and historical geology.

(Other references: Environmental Geology by C.W. Montgomery and regional geology resources like Geology of
the Himalaya, Karakoram, Hindukush in Pakistan can be useful for specific topics

.)

Group III

Business Administration

Marketing Management by Philip Kotler & Kevin Keller

 – Covers fundamental marketing concepts,

strategies, and case studies; useful for the marketing section. 
Management by Stephen P. Robbins & Mary Coulter

 – A comprehensive management textbook

covering organizational management theories and practices (planning, organizing, leading,
controlling). 
Human Resource Management by Gary Dessler (with South Asian edition by Biju Varkkey)

 –

Recommended for HR management topics like recruitment, training, performance appraisal, etc.,
included in the syllabus.

(Also consult Principles of Managerial Finance by Gitman for the finance section

, Operations Management

by Jay Heizer & Barry Render

 for production/operations, and Supply Chain Management by Chopra & Meindl

 for supply chain topics. Some local publications like a compiled book by Syed Ahsan Zohaib cover all sections

in one volume

.)

Public Administration

Public Administration: Concepts and Practices with Particular Reference to Pakistan by Dr. Sultan Khan

 – A detailed book covering administrative theories and the working of public institutions, with

examples from Pakistan’s governance. 
Public Administration by John M. Pfiffner (New York: Ronald Press)

 – A foundational text on public

administration, focusing on principles of administration, organizational structure, and management
of public agencies.

Governance & Public Policies

Implementing Public Policy by Michael Hill & Peter Hupe

 – Explores the process and challenges of

executing public policy, including models and case studies, useful for understanding policy
implementation. 
Governing the Ungovernable by Ishrat Husain

 – Focuses on governance issues in Pakistan’s

context, written by a former bureaucrat; covers public policy challenges and reforms in Pakistan’s
civil service, economy, and institutions.

(Also recommended: Governance: South Asian Perspective by Hasnat Abdul Hye

 which provides essays on

governance issues specific to South Asia, including Pakistan.)

Town Planning & Urban Management

Urban Planning: Theory and Practice by M. Pratap Rao

 – A comprehensive book on urban planning

principles, city design, zoning, and development control, covering theoretical and practical aspects of
urban management. 
Urban Growth Management for Sustainable Urbanization (Examples from Asia-Pacific Region) – A
collection of research papers and reports

 illustrating challenges and strategies in managing

urban growth, especially relevant for case studies in the region.

Group IV

History of Pakistan & India

Muslim Civilization in Indo-Pakistan by S.M. Ikram

 – Chronicles the history and culture of the

Indian subcontinent under Muslim rule, providing context from the early medieval period to the 19th
century. 
A New History of Indo-Pakistan by K. Ali

 – A two-volume history that covers South Asia from

ancient times through the post-independence era, concisely addressing the needs of CSS candidates
for Indo-Pak history.

(Other   useful   books   include  Pakistan:   The   Formative   Phase   1857–1948  by   K.B.   Sayeed

  for   detailed

understanding of the events leading to Pakistan’s creation, and  The Constitutional and Political History of
Pakistan by Hamid Khan for post-1947 history, as mentioned above.)

Islamic History & Culture

The Spirit of Islam by Syed Ameer Ali

 – A classic work that provides insight into Islamic history,

cultural heritage, and the contributions of Muslims to civilization. 
History of the Arabs by Philip K. Hitti

 – A comprehensive history of the Arab people, covering the

pre-Islamic era through the rise of Islam and the Caliphates, which gives background for Islamic
culture and civilization.

(Additionally, Islam and the World by Abul Hasan Ali Nadwi is recommended for understanding the impact of
Islam on global civilization

. Candidates often refer to university notes that cover the political, social, and

cultural history of the Muslim world to complement these readings.)

British History

History of England by P.C. Thomas

 – A two-part book covering British history from ancient times

up to the modern period; useful for the Paper I (earlier periods). 
English History 1914–1945 by A.J.P. Taylor

 – Focuses on early 20th-century British history (from

World War I through World War II), covering the political, social, and economic developments of that
era.

(For broader coverage, Mastering Modern British History by Norman Lowe is an excellent resource that spans
19th and 20th century British history

, and Kenneth O. Morgan’s works (e.g.,  The People’s Peace: British

History 1945-1990) cover post-WWII developments

.)

European History

Mastering Modern European History by Stuart Miller

 – Provides a well-structured overview of

European history from the French Revolution through the late 20th century, including analysis of
major events and movements. 
A History of Modern Europe (1789–1991) by H.L. Peacock

 – A concise book that covers key events in

modern European history, useful for quick revision of major wars, revolutions, and political changes
across Europe.

History of USA

A People’s History of the United States by Howard Zinn

 – Presents U.S. history through a grassroots

perspective, covering socio-economic aspects and often overlooked narratives from 1492 through
the late 20th century. 
History of the United States of America by Majumdar, Altab & Ahmad

 – A comprehensive volume

that covers U.S. history chronologically and is tailored for CSS candidates.

(It is also helpful to utilize online resources like  USHistory.org  for summaries of U.S. history

, and to watch

documentaries or lectures for a more engaging understanding of historical events.)

Group V

Gender Studies

50 Key Concepts in Gender Studies by Jane Pilcher & Imelda Whelehan

 – An accessible guide

explaining fundamental concepts and theories in the field of gender studies. 
Gender Studies by Amanullah Gondal

 – A comprehensive text covering the entire CSS Gender

Studies syllabus, including feminist theories, gender issues in Pakistan, and global gender debates.

(Additional reading: works by authors like Kamla Bhasin (Understanding Gender), Chimamanda Ngozi Adichie
(We Should All Be Feminists), and Pakistani scholars like  Rubina Saigol  or  Farzana Bari  can provide valuable
perspectives

.)

Environmental Sciences

Environmental Science: Earth as a Living Planet by Daniel B. Botkin & Edward A. Keller

 – Covers

ecological principles, environmental issues, and sustainability, aligning well with the CSS syllabus. 
Environmental Science: Working with the Earth by G. Tyler Miller

 – Introduces environmental

science concepts and human impacts on the environment in a reader-friendly manner.

(Candidates should also keep current with environmental reports and use resources like Pakistan’s Environment
Ministry website and UNEP publications for updated information

.)

Agriculture & Forestry

Agri-Forestry by Akhtar Abbas

 – A concise book focusing on agriculture and forestry topics,

combining essential concepts of crop production, soil science, and forest management relevant to
the CSS syllabus. 
Agriculture in Pakistan by Dr. Mohammad Hussain Khan

 – Discusses Pakistan’s agricultural sector,

policies, and issues in detail, providing local context and statistics which are useful for paper
examples.

(Regularly reviewing the Economic Survey of Pakistan’s agriculture chapter and forestry statistics can also be
beneficial

.)

Botany

Esau’s Plant Anatomy by Ray F. Evert & Susan E. Eichhorn

 – Covers plant structure and function

(cells, tissues, and organs of plants) which helps in understanding foundational botanical concepts. 
Cryptogamic Botany (Volumes I & II) by G.M. Smith

 – These volumes (Algae & Fungi; Bryophytes &

Pteridophytes) cover non-flowering plants and are useful for topics on lower plant forms and their
life cycles.

(For plant physiology and ecology sections, texts like Plant Physiology by Taiz & Zeiger

 and Fundamentals of

Ecology by Eugene Odum

 are also recommended.)

Zoology

Integrated Principles of Zoology by Hickman, Roberts & Larson

 – A comprehensive zoology

textbook covering animal diversity, structure, function, and evolution; useful for both invertebrate
and vertebrate zoology sections. 
Zoology (4th Edition) by Stephen Miller & John Harley

 – An introductory zoology text that covers

classification and characteristics of animal phyla along with basic anatomy and physiology of
animals.

English Literature

History of English Literature by Legouis & Cazamian (or David Daiches)

 – Provides an extensive

history of English literature, covering major literary periods, movements, and authors from Old
English to the modern era. 
A Critical History of English Literature by Dr. Muhammad Sadiq – A book often recommended for CSS
that summarizes various literary periods and offers critical commentary on major works, tailored to
the needs of students.

(Candidates should also familiarize themselves with the specific texts mentioned in the syllabus (e.g., works of
Shakespeare, Swift, Jane Austen, etc.) and may consult guides or notes for those particular works. Key reference
books like Edward Albert’s or Hudson’s histories of English literature can also be helpful

.)

Urdu Literature

Urdu Adab Ki Tareekh (History of Urdu Literature) by Prof. Noor-ul-Hasan Hashmi – A comprehensive
history of Urdu literature covering its evolutionary phases from the 16th century to contemporary
era. 
Urdu Tanqeed aur Jadeed Urdu Tanqeed by Dr. Wazir Agha – Discusses literary criticism and the
development of modern literary criticism in Urdu

, which helps in tackling questions on literary

movements and critical appraisal.

(Classical   works   by  Sheikh   Muhammad   Ikram  –  Aab-e-Kausar,  Rood-e-Kausar,  Mauj-e-Kausar  –   provide
historical context of Urdu literature’s development

. Additionally, reading essays of prominent Urdu critics and

poets (e.g., Mohammad Hussain Azad, Hali, Faiz) can enrich your answers.)

Group VI

Law

Pakistan Penal Code (1860) by M. Mahmood (commentary)

 – A detailed commentary on the PPC

1860, explaining various sections of criminal law in Pakistan. 
The Code of Criminal Procedure (1898) by Shaukat Mahmood

 – Explains the CrPC provisions and

procedures for criminal trials; important for understanding procedural law.

(Other relevant references: Law of Evidence by Justice (R) Khalil ur Rahman Khan (based on M. Monir’s work) for
Qanun-e-Shahadat

, and Civil Procedure Code by Aamir Raza Khan for CPC 1908

. It is also crucial to have

the bare acts of these laws for quick reference.)

Constitutional Law

Comparative Constitutional Law by Hamid Khan & M.W. Rana

 – Covers constitutional principles

with comparisons between various countries’ constitutions, and includes Pakistan’s constitutional
framework. 
Constitutional and Political History of Pakistan by Hamid Khan

 – (Also listed under Pakistan Affairs)

Provides insight into the making and development of Pakistan’s constitutions (1956, 1962, 1973) and
major constitutional cases

.

(Additionally, reading actual texts of world constitutions (UK, USA, France, etc.) and Selected Constitutions of the
World by Dr. Sultan Khan

 can be helpful for Paper II of Constitutional Law.)

International Law

International Law by Malcolm N. Shaw

 – A comprehensive and authoritative text on public

international law covering sources, statehood, diplomacy, use of force, human rights, etc. 
International Law by J.G. Starke

 – A classic book that provides clear explanations of international

law principles, often recommended for beginners to grasp fundamentals.

(For case studies and advanced topics,  Cases and Materials on International Law by D.J. Harris is useful

.

Also, Principles of Public International Law by Ian Brownlie is another authoritative source.)

Muslim Law & Jurisprudence

Muslim Law and Jurisprudence by Syed Aatir Rizvi

 – A concise book covering Islamic personal laws

(marriage, divorce, inheritance, etc.) and jurisprudential principles, as per the CSS syllabus. 
An Introduction to the Study of Islamic Law by Dr. Hussain Hamid Hassan

 – Explores the principles

of Islamic jurisprudence (Usul al-Fiqh) and the evolution of Islamic legal thought, useful for the
theoretical portion of the syllabus.

(Also recommended: Islamic Law of Inheritance by Hamid Khan for detailed understanding of inheritance laws in
Islam

.)

Mercantile Law

Mercantile Law by M.C. Kuchhal

 – A comprehensive book covering contract act, sale of goods,

partnership, negotiable instruments, etc., providing foundational concepts and illustrations. 
Business Law by Khalid Mehmood Cheema

 – A locally authored book that covers the Pakistani

statutes on contracts and mercantile matters in an easy-to-understand manner, aligned with the CSS
syllabus.

(Candidates should also be aware of key provisions of relevant acts: Contract Act 1872, Sale of Goods Act 1930,
Partnership Act 1932, Negotiable Instruments Act 1881, Companies Ordinance/Act, etc. Referring to the bare acts
or a compendium of business laws can be very useful for precise legal provisions.)

Criminology

Criminology: Theories, Patterns, and Typologies by Larry J. Siegel

 – Covers classical and modern

theories of crime, types of crimes, and the criminal justice system; a good introductory text for the
subject. 
Understanding Criminology by Sandra Walklate

 – Focuses on contemporary criminological

perspectives and debates, helping to address questions on crime and society with up-to-date
examples.

(It may also help to review Pakistan-specific crime data and laws (e.g., juvenile justice laws) and consult books like
New Perspectives in Criminology by J.E. Conklin for additional theory

.)

Philosophy

Philosophy Made Simple by Richard H. Popkin & Avrum Stroll

 – An introductory text that explains

key philosophical ideas and arguments in simple language, covering both Western and some
Eastern philosophy basics. 
A History of Muslim Philosophy (Vol. I & II, ed. M.M. Sharif)

 – A collection of scholarly essays on the

development of philosophical thought in the Muslim world, including figures like Al-Farabi, Al-
Ghazali, Ibn Sina, and others.

(For Western philosophy, Bertrand Russell’s History of Western Philosophy can be a useful overview. Also, Islamic
Philosophy by M. Saeed Sheikh gives a concise picture of major Muslim philosophers

. Utilizing the Stanford

Encyclopedia of Philosophy for specific topics is highly recommended for quick reference and clarification

.)

Group VII

Journalism & Mass Communication

Mass Communication: Theory and Practice by Umair Khan (JWT) – A comprehensive book covering
media theories, advertising, public relations, and the Pakistani media landscape, tailored for CSS
candidates. 
Journalism for All by Dr. Mehdi Hasan – A fundamental book (available in both Urdu and English) that
introduces the principles of journalism, news reporting and writing, and the role of media, with a
focus on Pakistan’s media evolution.

Psychology

Understanding Psychology by Robert S. Feldman

 – An introductory psychology textbook covering

all major topics (learning, personality, abnormal psychology, etc.) in an easy-to-grasp manner. 
Psychology (CSS Edition) by Tayyab Shahid (JWT)

 – A concise book that follows the FPSC syllabus

closely, providing exam-oriented explanations of psychological concepts and Pakistani case

(Another good reference is Psychology: Themes and Variations by Wayne Weiten for a more detailed study. Also,

Geography

Human Geography: People, Place, and Culture by H.J. de Blij and Alexander B. Murphy

 – Covers

human geography topics such as demographics, cultural patterns, urbanization, and economic
activities with case studies. 
Physical Geography by H.J. de Blij & Peter O. Muller

 – Focuses on physical geography including

climatology, geomorphology, hydrology, biogeography, and maps, which aligns with the physical
geography part of the syllabus.

(A local publication like Comprehensive Geography by Jahangir Sanpal

 is also handy for covering regional

geography of Pakistan. Candidates should use atlases and practice map-drawing for geographical distributions.)

Sociology

Introduction to Sociology by Abdul Hameed Taga & Abdul Aziz Taga

 – A book that covers all major

sociological concepts, theories, and social issues, organized as per syllabus requirements (popular
among CSS aspirants). 
Sociology by C.J. Horton & Chester L. Hunt

 – A classic text introducing fundamental sociological

theories and an analysis of social institutions, processes, and change, providing a strong conceptual
base.

(For   additional   reading,  Down   to   Earth   Sociology  by   James   Henslin   compiles   insightful   essays   on   various

. Using a CSS-specific sociology guide (e.g., by JWT) can help in quick revisions and MCQ

practice.)

Anthropology

Cultural Anthropology: The Human Challenge by William A. Haviland et al.

 – An accessible

introduction to cultural anthropology, exploring concepts like culture, kinship, religion, and language
with cross-cultural examples.

 – A comprehensive text that

Punjabi

Dugngian Sochaan by Akhtar Husain Jafferi

 – A book on Punjabi literature and poetry (in Punjabi

language), often recommended to cover the classical and modern Punjabi literary themes. 
Punjab Rang by Qamar Husnain Qadri & Faisal Hayat Jappa

 – Provides an overview of Punjabi

syllabus.

(Candidates should be comfortable with writing Punjabi in Shahmukhi script and be familiar with famous works of

Sindhi

Sindhi Adab Jo Jadeed Mutaleo by Mukhtiar Ahmed Mallah

 – Discusses modern Sindhi literature

and literary movements, as well as key writers/poets in Sindhi. 
Sindhi Adab Ji Mukhtasar Tareekh (Mukhtasar Jaizo) by Akbar Laghari

 – A concise history of Sindhi

literature, covering its evolution from early periods to the present.

(Ensure familiarity with the Sindhi script and famous Sindhi poets like Shah Abdul Latif Bhittai and Sachal
Sarmast. Reading Sindhi short stories and essays can also help for the subjective part.)

Pushto

Pohana (Pashto–Urdu edition) by Hanif Khalil

 – A comprehensive guide for Pashto language and

literature, written in Urdu for easy understanding, covering Pashto grammar, vocabulary, and literary
topics. 
The Pathans by Sir Olaf Caroe

 – While not a language textbook, this book gives historical and

cultural background on Pashtun society which can deepen one’s understanding of the context of
Pashto literature and famous Pashto literary works.

(Also study the poetry of Khushal Khan Khattak and Rehman Baba in Pashto, as well as available Pashto academy
notes. Having a Pashto dictionary and practicing translation both from and to Pashto is important.)

Balochi

Balochi–English Dictionary by Dr. Naseer Dashti

 – Useful for building vocabulary and

understanding classical Balochi texts, since translation is a part of the exam. 
Tareekh-e-Balochi Adab (History of Balochi Literature) by Zahoor Shah Hashmi – Covers the evolution
of Balochi literature, major poets, and literary works; helpful for attempting literary questions in
Balochi.

Arabic

A New Arabic Grammar of the Written Language by J.A. Haywood & H.M. Nahmad – A comprehensive
grammar reference that covers classical Arabic syntax and morphology, useful for mastering
translation and grammar questions. 
Tareekh Adab-e-Arabi (History of Arabic Literature) by Ahmad Hasan Zayyat (Urdu) – A history of Arabic

important literary figures/works in each era.

(To prepare well, practice translating unseen Arabic passages and poetry. Study the prescribed texts (poems and
